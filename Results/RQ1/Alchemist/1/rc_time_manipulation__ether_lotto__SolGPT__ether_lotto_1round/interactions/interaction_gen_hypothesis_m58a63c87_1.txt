The original contract uses `block.difficulty` as a source of randomness, while the mutant replaces it with `block.prevrandao`. In Ethereum after The Merge, `block.difficulty` returns 0, while `block.prevrandao` returns the actual random value from the beacon chain. This changes the random number generation and thus the outcome of the `if (random == 0)` condition.

**Hypothesis:** The mutant changes the source of randomness for determining the winner. Therefore, a test case that calls `play()` multiple times with the same `block.timestamp` and compares the winner selection (whether the bank receives the fee and the player receives the pot) against the expected behavior under `block.difficulty` should kill the mutant. The mutant, using `block.prevrandao`, produces a non-zero random value, causing the player to lose. A test that calls `play()` on a network where `block.difficulty` is 0 and expects the player to win (i.e., receive `pot - FEE_AMOUNT` and the bank to receive `FEE_AMOUNT`) will pass on the original but fail on the mutant, because the mutant will send the entire pot to the bank instead.
