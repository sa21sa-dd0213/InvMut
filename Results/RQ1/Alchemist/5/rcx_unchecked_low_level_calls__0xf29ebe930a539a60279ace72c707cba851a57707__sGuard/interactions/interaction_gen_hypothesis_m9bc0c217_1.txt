Let’s reason through this step by step.

The original `go()` function sends the entire `msg.value` to `target` via a low-level `call`. After that, it transfers the contract’s entire balance to `owner`.  

In the mutant, `msg.value` is replaced by `msg.value - 1`. This means the contract will send **1 wei less** to `target` than what was received in the transaction.  

Because the transfer to `owner` sends `address(this).balance` (the entire contract balance after the `call`), and the `call` sent 1 wei less, the contract will retain 1 wei after the function completes.  

A test that sends exactly 1 wei in the transaction would be particularly revealing:  
- Original: sends 1 wei to `target`, then transfers 0 wei to owner → contract balance becomes 0.  
- Mutant: sends 0 wei (`1 - 1 = 0`) to `target`, then transfers contract’s full balance (1 wei) to owner → contract balance becomes 0 as well, so this might not catch it.  

But if you send **2 wei**:  
- Original: sends 2 wei to `target`, contract balance becomes 0 → owner gets 0.  
- Mutant: sends 1 wei to `target`, contract still has 1 wei left → owner gets 1 wei.  

Checking the **final balance of the owner** or the **final balance of the contract** after the call will expose the difference.

**Hypothesis:**  
A test that sends a specific amount of ether (e.g., 2 wei) to `go()`, then checks that the contract’s balance is zero after execution, should pass on the original but fail on the mutant because the mutant leaves 1 wei in the contract.
