The original code does:

```solidity
pot += msg.value;
```

The mutant changes it to:

```solidity
pot += msg.value + 1;
```

This means that every time `play()` is called with exactly 10 wei (the required `TICKET_AMOUNT`), the pot is incremented by 11 wei instead of 10 wei.

Now, when a player wins (`random == 0`), the contract sends:
- `FEE_AMOUNT` (1 wei) to the bank
- `pot - FEE_AMOUNT` to the player

In the original, after one play, `pot = 10`, so winner gets `10 - 1 = 9` wei.  
In the mutant, after one play, `pot = 11`, so winner gets `11 - 1 = 10` wei — one more than they should.

This means the contract balance will be insufficient to pay out correctly over multiple rounds, or the payout will be wrong. The key invariant broken is: **the pot should always equal the sum of all `msg.value` (10 wei each) from players**.

The mutant increments the pot by `msg.value + 1` instead of `msg.value`. Therefore, a test case that calls `play()` with exactly 10 wei, then checks that the contract’s balance equals exactly 10 wei (or that the pot state variable equals exactly 10 wei) before any payout, should detect the mutant because the mutant will show a balance/pot of 11 wei instead of 10 wei.
